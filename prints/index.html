Linocut Relief Editions

Studio Process · Relief Carving

Carving Light from the Shadow of the Block

Linocut is a demanding subtractive printmaking discipline where every cut into the block is permanent and deliberate.

01
Drawing & Inversion:

The composition is drafted and transferred in mirror-image onto the linoleum matrix, balancing positive black forms with negative spatial rhythm.

02
Hand Gouging:

Hand-carved across dozens of hours into the block. Raised untouched surfaces retain ink while carved recesses preserve pure paper.

03
Inking & Burnishing:

Artist-grade ink is rolled in smooth layers. Paper is aligned and hand-burnished under firm, even pressure.

Ordinary Objects 1

Theme Micro spaces in macro environment
Year Carved|2021; Printed|2026
Frame Dimensions 61 × 52 cm
Print Dimensions 44.2 × 29.2 cm
Edition Limited Edition of 8
Time Taken 45 hours · Hand carving and printing

Ordinary Objects 1 is a linocut that grew out of the Covid period, when being homebound changed the way familiar surroundings were seen. Objects passed countless times began to occupy the small spaces of home, micro spaces within a macro environment. The piece observes a lamp and a few mismatched mugs closely, and honours the printmaking process itself, where the final image comes through adjustments and unsuccessful impressions rather than one perfect attempt.

Cyanotype Editions

Studio Process · Sun Printing

Painting with Sunlight & Prussian Blue

First formulated in 1842 by Sir John Herschel, cyanotype is an alternative photographic printmaking process using natural sunlight to transform minerals into deep indigo blues.

01
Sensitizing the Paper:

Watercolor paper is hand-coated in subdued light with an emulsion of ferric ammonium citrate and potassium ferricyanide, then cured in darkness.

02
Solar Exposure:

Botanical specimens and hand-cut silhouette forms are placed in direct contact atop the dry paper and exposed to midday natural ultraviolet rays.

03
Water Wash & Oxidation:

The exposed sheet is thoroughly washed in cold running water to clear unexposed emulsion, deepening into its rich Prussian blue tone through natural air oxidation.
